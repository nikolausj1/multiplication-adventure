# Metadata v3

Written to answer the Guideline 4.3(a) Design: Spam rejection of 1.0 (6) on
2026-08-13. Apple said the app "shares a similar binary, metadata, and/or
concept as apps submitted to the App Store by other developers, with only minor
differences," and did not answer the follow-up asking which apps it was matched
against (11 days of silence as of 2026-08-24).

The strategy is to make the next submission demonstrably NOT a repackaged
template, in the three places that actually carry weight: the binary, the
reviewer notes, and the screenshots. Metadata copy is a smaller lever and is
tuned rather than rewritten.

## Unchanged, deliberately

- **App name: Multiplication Adventure.** Justin's call. It is in the DNA of the
  app, 4.3(a) is about binary/assets/concept rather than titles, and descriptive
  names are common among legitimate apps.
- **Keywords.** They are invisible to users, they exist for discovery, and no
  reviewer judges spam by the keyword field. Changing them would cost
  findability for no defensive benefit.
- **The description body.** It is already specific and personal: named worlds,
  named guardians, real mechanics. No template ships that copy. Only additions
  below.

### Subtitle (25/30)

### Promotional text (162/170)

    Seven math worlds taken over by seven guardians that can be defeated by knowing multiplication facts. No ads, no tracking, offline. Built by one dad for his kids.

Justin's wording, trimmed to fit. Drops "the fun way", which was the most
template-sounding phrase in the listing, and ends on provenance, which is the
strongest anti-spam signal available and is true.

### Description (2884/4000)

Three edits to the existing copy, nothing removed: a provenance paragraph after
the opening line, "hand-painted" struck, and a closing AND THEN IT ENDS section.

### App Review notes (2903/4000)

Structure matters as much as content. Substance leads; the rejection is a short
factual note near the end, not the opening. Opening with a defence against a
spam accusation makes the review about the accusation. Opening with what the app
is makes it about the app.

Deliberately absent: any offer to merge the two apps (Justin has ruled it out),
and any description of how the artwork was produced (no Apple rule requires it,
and 4.3 asks whether assets exist elsewhere, which is answered directly).

The Addition Adventure disclosure stays, framed confidently rather than
apologetically: two sons, different ages, different arithmetic. A reviewer
working a 4.3 case checks the account's other apps as a matter of course, so the
choice was never whether they find it, only whether they hear it from us first.
The only offer made is withdrawal of that record, which costs nothing today
because it is already on hold.
